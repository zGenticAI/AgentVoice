# Changelog

The notable changes in each version. The project follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/): until 1.0, a minor version may change settings or behaviour, and says so here.

## Unreleased

### Added

- Codex and OpenCode as your coding session, beside Claude Code: the same `zoom-meeting` channel and meeting-assistant, set up by files committed in this folder. See docs/codex.md and docs/opencode.md.
  - Codex CLI 0.171.0 or later, pull only: questions reach it only through the meeting-assistant, and it gets no events and no Telegram; the minutes come in the meeting-assistant's report. `.codex/config.toml` registers the channel for this folder (with `tool_timeout_sec = 900` for `next_question`'s longest wait), makes `join_meeting`, `set_brief` and `slides_control` ask every time and the other eight tools never, and sets the floor as a permission profile, which Codex's sandbox enforces on every command in this folder and for the home folder's credential folders, Codex's and OpenCode's own included, with no network for commands. On Linux, the setup script refuses a home folder inside `/tmp` or `$TMPDIR`, where Codex leaves placeholder files for the floor. `.codex/agents/meeting-assistant.toml` is the agent. Codex can't limit its tools, so it has the session's shell: the file says how to turn the shell off, and what is lost then. `scripts/install-codex.sh` checks Codex, whether it loads this folder's config, and what to do if not, and writes nothing.
  - OpenCode 2.3.2 or later (V2, `@opencode/cli`), with push: a question no meeting-assistant takes, and each meeting event, is posted into your session through OpenCode's background service, once the channel knows which session is yours. `opencode.json` registers the channel, asks before `join_meeting`, `set_brief`, `slides_control` and `npm run slides`, sets the floor as policies on OpenCode's `read` tool, and limits the meeting-assistant to its six tools and `read`. The channel posts only into a session that isn't a subagent's, and only to the background service it runs in: never to a service whose registration was left behind, or to a server of its own. It posts nothing while OpenCode has an "Allow always" saved for what the config makes ask, which it checks before every post. The config also takes the channel's tools away from OpenCode's built-in General subagent. `scripts/install-opencode.sh` writes your own, gitignored `.opencode/opencode.json`: the channel's absolute path, and with `--source`, the folders outside this one the meeting-assistant may read. Your OpenCode session can answer a posted question while nobody is at the screen, until you stop its service (`opencode service stop`). The channel warns when OpenCode says it is V1, older than 2.3.2, or another major version.
  - `AGENTVOICE_CLIENT`, set by each client's committed config (`codex` or `opencode`; unset means Claude Code). The channel refuses any other value, ignores one in `.env`, and, for Codex and OpenCode, ignores `ZOOM_JOIN_TELEGRAM_CHATS` with a warning.
- A question no linked session can take (no meeting-assistant is taking questions, and the session can't be sent one) is given up at once: the assistant says it can't reach your notes then, instead of after a minute, and `why_not_answered` says the meeting-assistant wasn't running. With several sessions linked, it first goes on to the one that can be sent it and was used most recently. A Claude Code session can always be sent one, so with Claude Code alone nothing changes.
- Presenting slides in a meeting, the second half of the slides feature. `npm run join -- "<link>" --slides <deck>.script.md [--from <slide>]`, or `join_meeting`'s `slides` and `slides_from`, sends the assistant in with a deck you prepared. It waits for someone to say "<name>, start the presentation" (or for you), then shares a silent slides page as its screen beside its camera, asks once whether everyone can see it, and says your script, word for word, one sentence at a time, changing slides only once a slide's last sentence has played and the room is quiet. A question by name is answered, then "Back to <title>." and the sentence it stopped at; side talk gets "Shall I carry on?"; anyone who says the name may pause, carry on, go back, go to a slide and stop sharing; only you can end it for good, wrap up, or release your own pause. "<name>, please leave" always wins, and blanks the slides before the goodbye. It never talks on when it can't hear. Your controls: `slides_status` and `slides_control` in the channel, and `npm run slides -- status` and `control <action>`. Your session gets `presentation` events on changes and problems, and `meeting_ended` says how far the deck got. Answers during a deck know the outline and the slide on screen, earlier narration is folded in the transcript Claude answers from, the minutes mention the presentation in one sentence, and `why_not_answered` says when a line held the slides. A meeting without slides is unchanged. See docs/presenting.md.
- `SLIDES_PLATFORMS` in `.env`: the platforms presenting is on for, none unless set. Add one only once the share test has passed in a meeting you host there. A join with slides on any other platform is refused before any bot is created, with a message that names the platform, docs/share-test.md and the setting.

- `npm run slides`, the first half of presenting slides: preparing and checking a deck, on your computer. `prepare <deck.pdf> [--exclude <pages>] [--label <name>]` turns a PDF, exported from PowerPoint, Keynote, Google Slides, Canva or anything else, into a prepared deck in `data/decks/`: one 1280x720 image per page, each page's text and title, and a contact sheet that shows every slide as a meeting will see it. It writes a script beside the PDF, with a section per slide for you, or your Claude Code session, to write what Alex will say, word for word. Prepared again after you change the deck, it updates the script without touching a written paragraph: it renumbers the sections of slides that moved, found by their text or their picture, excluded ones too, says which new pages have no section yet, gives the section of a slide no longer in the deck `exclude: yes` (such a slide never comes back: a new one on its number gets a section of its own), and marks each section whose slide changed, in its text or its picture, `check: changed` until you have read it, an excluded one too. `check <deck>.script.md` checks the script against its deck, estimates its length and refreshes the deck's own script's contact sheet; it reads only a file of that name, quotes none of its front matter, and compares the slides' figures only for the script prepare wrote for the deck. `list` and `delete` manage the decks. None of them needs a key or the network. Other formats are refused, by what is in the file rather than its name, with how to export a PDF from it, and so is a PDF that needs a password to open. The PDF is read by pdf.js in a child process whose environment holds no API keys, stopped after 3 minutes. Decks are readable only by you, checked strictly whenever they are read, and deleted `DECK_RETENTION_DAYS` (30 unless set) after they were last used. Presenting a deck in a meeting comes in a later version. See docs/presenting.md.
- A code of conduct: the Contributor Covenant 2.1.
- `npm run join -- "<link>" --share-test`, a test in a meeting you host of the one thing the slides feature depends on: whether the assistant's camera page, which carries its voice and hearing, keeps running while the bot shares a screen, replaces the share and stops it. The assistant joins as usual, shares a silent test page step by step, and asks you in the terminal what you see and hear; then it leaves as on "please leave" (or stays, with `--keep`), and prints a report to paste back to your Claude Code session, with a verdict for the platform. `--json` also writes the raw step records (readable only by you). Nothing printed or written holds a key, a token, an address, the meeting link or the bot's id. Only `npm run join` can start it, never `join_meeting`; the test page and its link are on the public port only while that meeting has a share on, with the share's own token. A meeting joined without it is unchanged. See docs/share-test.md.
- `npm run flux-probe`, a test with your real keys of what Deepgram Flux does with backchannels ("mm-hm", "yeah"), interruptions, noise, and the end of a stream, to tune when talking over the assistant stops it. It streams test clips (spoken in the service's voice, made in code, and with `--mic`, `--files` or `--nonverbal`, your own) to Flux exactly as the service does, and prints a report to paste back to your Claude Code session: what Flux sent for each, what the assistant would have done, where that is wrong, and suggested values for the constants and word lists, which it never applies. It changes nothing, writes nothing unless `--json` names a file (readable only by you), and never prints a key. See docs/flux-probe.md.
- A brief for one meeting: `npm run join -- "<link>" --brief <file>`, or `join_meeting`'s `brief`. That meeting answers, and its minutes are written, from it instead of the shared brief (`data/brief.md`), and no other meeting sees it, even when `set_brief` changes the shared brief meanwhile. It is kept in the voice service's memory only, until the meeting is over. `get_profile` takes the meeting's key, and gives that meeting's brief; when it can't be had (the meeting is over, say) it gives none, never the shared one in its place. The join note says which brief the meeting uses. The control `/health` and the bridge now say what the service can do (`features`), so a newer `npm run join` or channel refuses a brief to an older service, which would ignore it.
- Ask your Claude Code session why the assistant didn't answer something: the channel's new `why_not_answered` tool gives, for the latest lines of a live meeting (10, or up to 50), what the assistant did with each and why, by its attention rules. It is kept in memory only, never in a file or the log, goes when the meeting ends or at once on "please leave" or a removal, and only your session can read it. The meeting-assistant agent doesn't get it.
- The speaker probe, a test mode for setting up speaker names: with `SPEAKER_PROBE=1` in `.env`, a meeting joined with `npm run join -- "<link>" --speaker-probe` measures how the meeting platform's participant events (who joined, who is speaking, from Recall) line up with the turns the assistant hears. It keeps one file of counts and timings per meeting in `data/speaker-probe/`, with no names or words, deleted with the transcript, and none for a meeting it was asked to leave. `npm run speaker-report` pools them by platform. Off by default; `join_meeting` never turns it on; for test meetings whose participants have agreed (see docs/speaker-probe.md). The control `/health` and the `/api/join` answer say whether it is on (`speaker_probe`).

### Changed

- Logs and printed lines say "coding session" where Codex or OpenCode applies as well: the bridge's lines (such as `[bridge] Questions now go to coding session #N`), the last line of the flux probe's and the share test's reports, `prepare`'s next step, the slides join note, and the description of the fast tier's tool that hands a question to your session, which Claude reads. The service's start-up lines say "the zoom-meeting channel". `get_transcript` says it only reads (`readOnlyHint`). Otherwise, what the channel tells Claude Code is unchanged.
- `/api/join` reads up to 352,384 bytes of a request (it was 112,384), for a presentation script of up to 40,000 characters beside the brief, for every meeting. The control `/health` and the bridge add `slides` to `features`, and the control socket has `/api/slides`. The public port answers `/slides`, `/slides.js` and `/slides-link` only while a meeting's slides are being shared: otherwise they are not found, as before.
- A meeting with slides gets its own consent line and chat notice, which say it will present slides when asked and how, and is left after a longer silence (the script's minutes plus 15, from 15 to 60 minutes). Once `SLIDES_PLATFORMS` lists a platform, the service also keeps the audio of the slides' fixed lines in `data/tts-cache`.

- `npm install` now also installs pdf.js (`pdfjs-dist`) and, as an optional dependency, the native `@napi-rs/canvas`, about 73 MB together, to prepare slide decks. The voice service and the Claude Code channel never load them. `npm install --omit=optional` leaves the canvas out on a computer that never prepares a deck.
- The voice service creates `data/decks` (readable only by you) when it starts, and its hourly retention sweep deletes the decks there not used for `DECK_RETENTION_DAYS`, logging counts only.
- The committed `.claude/settings.json` also stops Claude Code sessions reading `data/decks`, and docs/claude-code.md says not to allow `Bash(npm run slides:*)` or `Bash(npm run slides -- check:*)`: `prepare` reads any PDF it is given, and `check` opens the file it is given itself, where deny rules don't apply. `.gitignore` keeps out `decks/`, where the docs now keep your PDFs and scripts, and a PDF or script at the top of the folder.
- Briefs are limited to 16,000 characters, `set_brief` included: a longer one is refused, never cut, since its end usually says what must not be shared. A `data/brief.md` edited by hand past the limit is not used at all until it is shortened: meetings without a brief of their own answer as if there were none, and the join note, the service log (its length only) and `get_profile` say why. Before, `set_brief` took anything, and the shared brief was used however long it was.
- One websocket message may hold at most 1 MiB on the bridge and 64 KiB from the bot page; a larger one closes its connection. Before, ws's default of 100 MiB applied.
- Talking over the assistant pauses it first. It stops only for words, and carries on after a cough, a laugh, or "mm-hm", "yeah", "okay", "right" or "I see". It can now be talked over until the bot page has played the end of an answer. Before, it stopped at the first sound Deepgram turned into a word, and only while the answer was still going out to the page.
- The transcript says what the meeting heard the assistant say. When it is talked over, told never mind, or asked something new partway through an answer, when its page goes, or when ElevenLabs fails partway through a line, the line keeps only the words the bot page had played, ending in "(cut off)", and a line it never got to is left out. The minutes and the next answers go by it. Before, the transcript had every line in full as soon as the assistant started it.
- The consent line and the goodbye are protected until the bot page has played them to the end, as the service now follows the page's playback. Before, it went by when their audio had gone out, which could be earlier: when the page started 50 ms late, when ElevenLabs paused partway, or when an answer to a question asked before the consent line was still playing ahead of it.
- The bot leaves a meeting by itself sooner: 5 minutes after it got in if nobody else has joined (Recall's default was 20), and after 15 minutes of silence, counted once it has been in the call 10 minutes (the default was 60, after 20).
- Speech recognition reconnects by itself when Deepgram drops the stream, or when the stream sends nothing back for 5 seconds while the meeting's audio goes to it (a connection that died without closing), as soon as more of the meeting's audio comes in, then backing off, and sends the last 2 seconds said meanwhile first. A sentence cut off by the drop is kept as far as it got. The bot page says "can't hear the meeting" only once Deepgram has been unreachable for 5 seconds, or refused the key, and the message clears once speech recognition is back. Before, a dropped stream left the assistant deaf for the rest of the meeting.
- A meeting survives a drop of the bot page's connection. The page reconnects by itself within a minute, with the secret the service gave it, and the meeting goes on: the same transcript, and no second consent line unless the drop cut it off. What was said while the page was away is not heard, so a question asked then has to be asked again. Before, a dropped connection ended the meeting, and the bot left the call.
- The bot page reconnects by itself when its connection to the service drops, showing "Reconnecting…" meanwhile: it notices a connection that has stopped answering within 4 seconds, tries again within a quarter of a second, then less and less often, and stops after 75 seconds or when the service says the meeting is over. The assistant's voice it already had plays on. Before, a drop left it "Disconnected" for good.
- A meeting that really ended as its page disconnected (or one Alex was removed from) is still reported within seconds: the service asks Recall whether the bot has left, instead of waiting the minute a page has to come back.
- The time limit reached while the bot page is reconnecting still posts its line in the chat before the bot leaves.
- A meeting whose session can't start (a key not set, say) has its bot leave at once, and its page is told not to try again. Before, the bot stayed in the call, silent, until the page wait ran out.
- The last words before a meeting ends are transcribed: the sentence in progress is ended before the stream closes, so it reaches the transcript and the minutes (and a "please leave" among them is honoured). Before, it was lost.

### Fixed

- A `set_brief` whose file couldn't be written (a full disk, say) stopped the service as a crash, ending every meeting. It is now refused, with why.
- The `[minutes]` log lines named the minutes file, whose name holds the meeting's key, and a failure's message, which can name it too. They now name the bot, and a failure's code only; the owner's session still hears the reason in full.
- The `[retention]` log line gave a failure's message, which names the file it could not delete, and so the meeting's key. It now gives counts and the error's code only. One file the sweep can't delete no longer stops it: the others still go, and the next sweep tries again.
- A refused Deepgram connection (a wrong key, no credit left) no longer piles up the meeting's audio in memory for the rest of the meeting, and is not tried again.
- A hello written as a sentence of its own, then a question in the next sentence ("Hi, Alex. Can you tell me about pricing?", as speech recognition often writes it), was read as asking nothing, and the question was lost (#11). After a hello, or an apology ("Sorry, Alex. Can you repeat that?"), the question in the next sentence is now answered, unless that sentence is for the room or someone else ("Hi Alex. Can we start?", "Hi Alex. Sarah, can you share your screen?"). After thanks, praise, "yes" or a goodbye it still isn't ("Thanks Alex. Can you also check the budget?"). The cost: after a hello, a question meant for the room that the rules don't know as the room's is answered ("Hi Alex. Is the API ready?"). `why_not_answered` now names a hello with nothing asked after it among the lines read as asking nothing. This applies to every meeting.
- In a meeting with slides, a filler after the name lost the slide command (#12): "Alex, um, start the presentation." was answered as a question and nothing was shared, and "Hello, Alex. Um, start the presentation." was ignored. Slide commands now read past "um", "uh", "er", "erm", "hmm" and "ah" wherever they are (between the name and the command, inside the phrase, at its end), take "oh", "well" and "actually" before the command as they take "okay" and "so", and read on into the next sentence when only those follow the name before a full stop ("Alex, um. Start the presentation.", "Alex, okay. Next slide."). A line that is not a command is still not one: "Alex, um, next slide, and what does it cost?" is answered. Before the presentation has first started, the assistant's answers also know it hasn't, and that "<name>, start the presentation" starts it. The leave rule reads as before.
- "Alex, uh, never mind." was not a cancel: the cancel rule skipped "um", "uh", "er", "erm" and "hmm" before the name but not after it. It now skips them after the name too, in every meeting. The name corpus reads the same.
- In a meeting with slides, the share stopped in questions after the last slide while the room was still talking to Alex by name, with nothing said, and nothing in the log to say why (#13). A line said to Alex by name that asks nothing ("Thanks, Alex.", "Alex?", "Alex, never mind.") now counts as something said to it, as a question or a slide command does: the two minutes in questions start again. Once two minutes pass with nothing said to Alex by name, it waits until the room is quiet, says once "I'll stop sharing my slides for now. Say "Alex, start the presentation" to bring them back.", and stops sharing. A line said to it by name before the share has stopped, even over that line, keeps the slides up; other talk over the line stops the line, and the share still stops. While it can't hear the meeting or its page is away, nobody can be heard talking to it, so it doesn't stop for this, and the two minutes start again once it can (before, the share stopped regardless). Each stop now says why, in the service's log and in the note of the `stopped` event your session gets: someone in the meeting, your stop or end, or the two minutes in questions, with how to share again (`slides_control` `start`, or "Alex, start the presentation"). The log lines of the presentation's events carry their notes, which are fixed text. With `SLIDES_PLATFORMS` set, the new line's audio is kept in `data/tts-cache` with the other slides lines.

### Security

- The `hono` and `@hono/node-server` packages, which the MCP SDK the Claude Code channel is built on depends on, are updated to versions without the advisories GitHub reported (GHSA-qv5m-9pm6-hqp3, GHSA-jpvv-r467-5p4w, GHSA-4m76-xfj5-7975, GHSA-h7mg-cq63-vhpq, GHSA-3gg2-335m-rxc6, GHSA-46wx-v44x-9hh2, GHSA-4h7h-rv7c-7m2v). They were not reachable here: the channel uses the SDK's stdio transport, and Hono only serves its HTTP one.

## 0.1.0

The first public release.

### Added

- A voice assistant that joins Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams meetings through Recall.ai, says that it is an AI, and answers questions said to it by name.
- Two tiers: quick answers from the meeting brief, and deeper ones from the owner's Claude Code session, through the `zoom-meeting` MCP channel and its meeting-assistant agent.
- "Please leave" and removal from the meeting make it leave and delete the meeting's transcript. "Never mind" stops it talking.
- Minutes after each meeting.
- Settings for the assistant's name, the owner and the company; a profile with the owner's context, sources and rules; and a bot page of your own.
- A public port for the meeting bot only, a control socket for everything else, and limits on meetings at once and meeting length.
- A launchd agent on macOS and a systemd user service on Linux.
- What the meeting sees, and what the log keeps, say no more than they must: when something fails, the bot page shows fixed words, never a provider's error, and the log names each bot and its platform, never the meeting's link.

# In a meeting

What the assistant does in a meeting, and the rules it goes by to decide whether a line was said to it. These pages call it Alex, its default name, and you, its owner, Sam; with another name, the rules are the same (see [Choosing the assistant's name](choosing-a-name.md)).

## Joining, leaving and stopping

When Alex joins, it says the consent line and posts the chat notice. Talking over the consent line doesn't cut it off, so everyone in the meeting when Alex joins hears how to make it leave (people who join later don't, so tell them yourself); a question asked during it is answered once it is over, and if someone says just "Alex?" during it, the 10 seconds for the next line count from when it is over. It leaves when someone says "Alex, please leave" (or "Alex? Please leave.", or "Alex, could you leave the meeting now?"): whatever it was saying stops at once, the consent line included, along with what the page still had queued to play, so the goodbye is heard straight away and in full, and the bot drops from the call half a second after the goodbye has played. From the moment of the request it records and answers nothing more, sends no more of the meeting's audio to speech recognition, deletes its transcript (and any minutes of the meeting, including those of an earlier session if its page had reconnected), and writes no minutes. That holds even when the request is among the last words speech recognition delivers after the meeting has ended. Because that can't be undone, the rule is strict:
- The name has to be said **to** Alex: at the start of a sentence (perhaps after "hey", "thanks", "um", "no" or "excuse me"), or set off by commas ("I'm not comfortable with this, Alex, please leave."). Talk **about** Alex never counts: "I don't want Alex to leave the call.", "Should we ask Alex to leave the meeting?", "When does Alex drop off?".
- Only the exact name counts (and "Ionics", a mishearing, as the first word; with another name, the mishearings in `AGENT_NAME_MISHEARD`): "Alec, please hang up" doesn't.
- The request has to end the sentence, however politely put ("Alex, could you please leave the meeting?", "Alex, it's time to go."): "Alex, please leave a note in the chat" doesn't count. "Alex, I think you should leave." counts as a plain statement, but not "Alex, I don't think you should leave.", "Alex, do you have to leave?" or "Alex, before you have to leave, can you recap?".
- Right after someone said just "Alex?", or Alex asked something, an explicit "Please leave." or "Leave the meeting." on its own counts without the name. A bare "Can you leave now?" doesn't, since it may be said to a colleague.

The transcript and minutes it deletes are its own copies, in `data/`: what was already handed to your Claude Code session stays in that session's history (see the README's [Where meeting data goes](../README.md#where-meeting-data-goes)).

To stop Alex without making it leave, say "Alex, never mind" (or "Alex, stop", "Alex, cancel that", "Alex, don't worry about it", "Alex, leave it", "Never mind, Alex"). It stops talking at once, even the end of an answer the page is still playing, drops a question it is still checking with your session (the answer is still kept, so asking again gets it at once), and says nothing. A question that only contains such a word, such as "Alex, can you stop the recording?", is answered like any other. It doesn't stop the consent line, which is always said in full.

Removing Alex from the meeting (from the participant list, or from the waiting room) counts the same as "Alex, please leave": once Recall says the host removed the bot, the transcript and any minutes of the meeting are deleted and no minutes are written. See "How it ended" under [Minutes](minutes-and-data.md#minutes).

## If Alex doesn't answer

The service doesn't show what it heard or why it ignored a line, and its log never holds what was said in a meeting. To debug, read the meeting's transcript (`data/transcripts/`, see [Minutes](minutes-and-data.md#minutes)), check the line against the rules here, and look in the service's log: the Terminal running `npm start`, or `data/agent-voice.log` when it runs as a service (`scripts/install-service.sh` has launchd or systemd write it there; follow it with `tail -f data/agent-voice.log`):
- **Nothing anyone said is in the transcript**: speech isn't reaching Deepgram. Check the log shows `[audio] session started for bot ...` once the bot is in the call, then `[stt] connected to Deepgram`.
- **In the transcript, but not answered**: the line wasn't for Alex.
  - Alex answers when the name starts the sentence ("Alex, what's the timeline?", "Good morning Alex, can you ...") or is set off by commas ("So what do you think, Alex?", "Before we start, Alex, introduce yourself."), and something is asked: a question or a request. Words such as "sorry", "actually", "wait", "quick question", "one more thing" or "in terms of pricing" between the name and the question are fine, with or without commas. The question can also come in the next sentence ("Alex, quick question. How much is it?"), but not after a greeting, thanks or "Alex, hold on.", and not when that sentence is for someone else ("Hi Alex. Sarah, can you share your screen?", "Alex, no. What do you think, Sarah?").
  - Ignored on purpose: talking about Alex ("I asked Alex yesterday", "By the way, Alex is recording this", "Alex, Sam's assistant, is recording this, is that OK?", "Alex has it covered"); thanks and praise ("Thanks, Alex", "Thanks Alex, take care", "Alex, excellent answer"); asking Alex to wait or putting it off ("Alex, please wait", "Alex, give me a second", "Alex, we'll come back to you"), which lets a question Alex is still checking with your session carry on; and the host moving on ("Thanks Alex, who's next?", "Thanks Alex, next question.", "Cheers Alex, what do you think, Sarah?"). Telling Alex to stop ("Alex, please stop", "Alex, never mind") stops what it is doing without an answer (see [Joining, leaving and stopping](#joining-leaving-and-stopping)), but "Alex, forget that, what's the launch date?" is a new question, and Alex answers it.
  - When Alex ends an answer with a question, the next thing anyone says within 30 seconds is taken as the reply, without the name. Saying just "Alex?", or only announcing a question ("Alex, I have two questions.", "Alex, I wanted to ask about pricing."), makes the next thing said within 10 seconds go to Alex.
  - Some lines are missed on purpose, because catching them would make Alex answer remarks. Say the name again and they work. Speech recognition often leaves out commas and question marks, so: a name at the end counts without a comma only after "think", "say" and the like ("what do you think Alex"). With no comma after the name, "Alex is ..." or "Alex did ..." reads as a remark unless "it", "you", "we" or the like follows ("Alex is it ready" asks, "Alex is the API ready" doesn't), and "Alex has it ..." asks only as "Alex has it been ...". A yes/no question about a thing needs its question mark, even with the comma: "Alex, is the API ready?" asks, "Alex, is the API ready." doesn't. Someone else's name at the end needs its comma: without them, "Cheers Alex, what do you think, Sarah?" is answered. After thanks, one capitalised word on its own is taken as the next speaker called on, so "Thanks Alex, Sarah?" is ignored, and so is "Thanks Alex, Okta?" ("Alex, Okta?" asks). After "stop" or "forget that", a new question has to start like one: "Alex, forget that, what's the launch date?" is answered, "Alex, forget that, the launch date?" isn't. And after "Thanks Alex." or "Alex, wait." a question in the next sentence isn't taken as Alex's.
  - Speech recognition sometimes writes the name as Alec, Aleks or Alexa, so these count too, and so does "Ionics" as the first word or after "hey", "hi", "OK" or "so" ("Hey Ionics, ..."). Real first names such as Alexis or Alix don't, so Alex stays quiet when someone talks to a colleague. Leaving is stricter: see above. These lists are Alex's own: with another name you set your own (see [Choosing the assistant's name](choosing-a-name.md)).
  - Deepgram is told to listen out for the assistant's name, your name (`OWNER_NAME`) and your company (`COMPANY_NAME`, if set). Add other names and product words with `DEEPGRAM_KEYTERMS` in `.env`, comma-separated. Deepgram takes up to 100 terms and about 500 tokens in all; terms past that are left out, with a `[stt]` warning in the log.
- **Spoken to, but silent**: the log shows a `[turn]` line with the Claude or ElevenLabs error. The bot page, which the meeting sees as Alex's camera, only says "couldn't answer that" (or "can't hear the meeting" when speech recognition fails, or "can't start" when the meeting's session couldn't start, with a key not set, say): the error itself is only in the log.

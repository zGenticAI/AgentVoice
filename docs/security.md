# Ports and security

What can reach the voice service, and what keeps the meeting data on your computer private to you. These pages call the assistant Alex, its default name.

The service has two listeners, so what the internet can reach never depends on headers a proxy may or may not add:

| Listener | Where | Serves | For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `PORT` (8787), the public port | `HOST`: 127.0.0.1 unless set | the bot page (`/agent`) and its scripts, its audio (`/audio`), and `/health`, which says only `{"ok":true}`. With `SPEAKER_PROBE` on, also `/recall/`: Recall's participant events for a test meeting joined with `--speaker-probe`. During a share test only, also `/share-test` and `/share-test-link`: the test page and its link | the meeting bot, and Recall, through the tunnel |
| the control socket | `data/control.sock`, a Unix socket in the data folder (`CONTROL_SOCKET` to put it elsewhere) | `/api/join` (`npm run join`), `/bridge` (the Claude Code channel, `why_not_answered` included), `/api/share-test/status` and `/api/share-test/action` (`npm run join --share-test`), and `/health` with the details (whether a session is linked, how many meetings, `features`: what this service can do, and `speaker_probe`: whether `SPEAKER_PROBE` is on). No pages. | your user on this computer only |

- Point the tunnel at the public port. The control side has no port at all. Leave `HOST` unset unless the tunnel runs on another machine; with `HOST=0.0.0.0` the service warns at every start.
- **The bot's audio** needs `AGENT_PAGE_TOKEN` and the key of a meeting this service sent the bot into and that is still on: a made-up key, or one of a meeting that is over, is refused. One connection per meeting at a time. A page that drops may come back within a minute, and only with the secret the meeting's session gave it in its `ready` message (24 random bytes, compared in constant time, never logged); with it, it can also take the place of a connection that has stopped answering. Until the meeting's page has sent anything (a page sends nothing before its `ready`), a later try without the secret may also take its place, so a page whose first `ready` was lost can still come in; it still needs the page token and the meeting's key. Any other page gets 409, and so does a try older than the one let in. After "Alex, please leave" or a removal no page is let in, not even that one, and once the time limit is reached or the meeting is over, none is (403). The meeting keeps its place under `MAX_MEETINGS` meanwhile.
- **Recall's participant events** (`/recall/`, the [speaker probe](speaker-probe.md)) exist only with `SPEAKER_PROBE` on; otherwise the path is not found, like any other.
  - Each meeting joined with `--speaker-probe` gets a token of its own (32 random bytes, never the page token, never logged) in the address Recall is given, compared in constant time. It opens only that meeting, while it is on: a made-up key, another meeting's token, a meeting without the probe, one that is over or being left, and one whose measurements went with "please leave" are refused (403), and so is anything with an `Origin` header (Recall is not a browser). The token is checked before whether the service is stopping (503), so a stranger learns nothing from that.
  - One connection per meeting, and the newest wins: Recall tries again 30 times, 3 s apart, then gives up for good, so it must always get back in. Anything still arriving on the connection it replaced is ignored. At most one is let in a second (429).
  - A message may hold 64 KiB, and each connection is read for at most 100 messages a second: the rest are dropped and counted, unread. The service sends nothing but a ping every 30 s, and cuts a connection it hasn't heard from for 60 s. Refusals are logged at most once a minute, as a count.
  - The address, with its token, stays in Recall's bot record, as the bot page's does, and passes through your tunnel; it opens nothing once the meeting is over. Whoever holds it (Recall, or your tunnel provider) could take the connection's place and send events of their own: for the probe, that spoils only the measurements.
- **The share test's page and link** (`/share-test` and `/share-test-link`, the [share test](share-test.md)) open only for a meeting joined with `npm run join --share-test`, only while it has a share on, and only with that share's token. Otherwise they are not found (404), like any other path, whatever the token, so they say nothing when no test is running.
  - Each share has a token of its own (32 random bytes, never the page token, never logged), in the page's address Recall is given, and compared in constant time. It dies when the share is stopped, when the meeting ends, and when the service stops. A share that replaces another leaves the old token valid only until the new page links up, or 20 s.
  - The page goes out with `Cache-Control: no-store`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, and a policy that runs only its own inline script and style (by their SHA-256) and lets it connect only to its link on the public address. It makes no sound, never asks for a microphone or camera, and never touches `/audio`: the service checks the page at start.
  - One link per share, and the newest wins: the one before it is closed. A message may hold 4 KiB, must be JSON, and a link may send at most 20 a second; anything else closes it. The service sends it only a session number and frame numbers, never anything of the meeting.
  - The `Origin` the bot's browser sends on the link is kept, as its scheme and host only, for the report, and never logged.
  - Whoever holds a live share's address (Recall, or your tunnel provider) could load the test page or take its link's place: that shows them a number and a text ladder, and spoils only the test's measurements.
- **The control socket** is a Unix socket in the data folder, not a port on 127.0.0.1. Only your user can open it (the folder is 0700, the socket 0600), and no other user can listen in its place while the service is down, as anyone on this computer could on a port above 1024 and so catch the local token from the channel or `npm run join`. Both send the token only to a socket in a folder only you can open. No browser can open a Unix socket, so DNS rebinding has nothing to reach and no `Host` is checked; `/api/join` and `/bridge` still refuse anything with an `Origin` header (a web page's requests always have one) and need the local token, as a second line, and `/api/join` also needs JSON.
  - The details: `curl --unix-socket data/control.sock http://localhost/health` (the service prints this line, with the full path, when it starts). Its `features` list what this service can do beyond what every version could (now `meeting_brief`, a meeting's own brief, `why_not_answered`, what Alex did with a meeting's latest lines, and `share_test`, the screen-share test), and the service sends the same list first on every `/bridge` link. A newer `npm run join` or channel refuses what an older service would quietly ignore, such as a meeting's own brief, rather than send Alex in with the shared one.
  - `/api/share-test/status` and `/api/share-test/action`, which `npm run join --share-test` drives the test with, refuse anything with an `Origin` header and need the local token, as `/api/join` does, and answer only for a meeting joined with the test (404 for any other). `join_meeting` and the bridge can't start the test.
  - `/api/join` reads at most 112,384 bytes of a request (a meeting link, and a brief of up to 16,000 characters however JSON writes it), and only after the local token has been checked.
  - At start the service replaces a stale socket (one a service that did not stop cleanly left behind), and refuses to start if something other than a socket is at the path, or another voice service is running on it.
  - A socket's path can be 103 bytes at most on macOS (107 on Linux). If the data folder's path is longer than that allows, the service says so at start: set `CONTROL_SOCKET` in `.env` to a shorter path in a folder only you can open, such as `~/.agent-voice/control.sock` after `mkdir -m 700 ~/.agent-voice`. `npm run join` and the channel read the same `.env`.
- **The local token** is `data/local-token`: a random secret the service makes at its first start, readable only by you. `npm run join` and the zoom-meeting channel read it from the same data folder and send it with every request, so another program on this computer can't start bots or take the questions. Without it they say "start the voice service once to create it". To change it, delete the file and restart the service; the channel reads it again each time it relinks.
- **Message size:** one websocket message may hold at most 1 MiB on `/bridge` (the channel's largest is a brief, under 100 KB), 64 KiB on `/audio` (the bot page sends 2,560-byte frames), 64 KiB on `/recall/` (a participant event is under 2 KB) and 4 KiB on `/share-test-link`. A larger one closes its connection (1009) before the service reads the rest, so one link can't make the service hold up to ws's default of 100 MiB at once. A page whose connection closes that way is away, as after any drop.
- **Files:** the `data` folder is readable only by you (0700), and so are the shared brief (0600), the local token (0600), the control socket (0600) and `pending-bots.json` (0600). Transcripts, minutes, the speaker probe's measurements (`data/speaker-probe/`, its folder made 0700 again at every write) and the audio of the fixed lines are written readable only by you, and the service sets the folder, the brief, the token and the socket again at every start. A meeting's own brief is never written to a file: the service keeps it in memory, for that meeting, until its session is over, and the channel asks for it over `/bridge` (`get_brief`) when `get_profile` is given the meeting's key.
- **Prepared slide decks** (`data/decks/`, made by `npm run slides -- prepare`: see [Presenting slides](presenting.md)): the folder and each deck's folder are 0700, every file 0600, and one strict loader refuses a deck that isn't: a symbolic link inside the folder, a file it doesn't expect or that another user could read, a manifest with an unknown field, an image that isn't a 1280x720 WebP with the hash its manifest gives. Only a deck's id is ever made into a path, after it is checked. The service creates the folder at start, and deletes decks `DECK_RETENTION_DAYS` (30 unless set) after they were last used, and unfinished prepares after an hour, in its hourly sweep; it logs counts only, never a deck's id or label. The committed `.claude/settings.json` stops Claude Code sessions reading the folder.
- **Converting a PDF:** pdf.js reads it in a child process of `npm run slides`, whose environment is made from scratch (`PATH`, `HOME` and a temporary folder of its own), so none of the API keys `npm run slides` loaded from `.env` are in it; a test keeps it from importing the modules that load `.env`. It never runs the PDF's JavaScript or fetches what the PDF links to, its output is read as strict JSON lines (its own messages are never shown), and it is killed, with anything it started, after 3 minutes, the only hard limit on its time and memory. Every slide image is encoded by us. It is not a sandbox: the child runs as you, so code that broke out of pdf.js could still read your files, `.env` and every API key in it included. The voice service and the channel never load pdf.js or the native canvas.

## Limits on cost

- `MAX_MEETINGS` (default 3): how many meetings Alex is in at once. A join beyond it, with `npm run join` or `join_meeting`, is refused: "Already in 3 meetings, the most MAX_MEETINGS allows." (with how many of them are ending). A meeting holds its place from the join until its bot is out of the call: after its page disconnects, through the minute in which a page may come back, and until Recall takes the request to leave (see [Minutes](minutes-and-data.md#minutes), "If the page disconnects"), so a meeting ending frees its place within seconds when Recall says it has ended, otherwise about a minute later. A bot whose page never connects (the tunnel was down, say) is asked to leave the call after 15 minutes, which frees its place.
- `MAX_MEETING_MINUTES` (default 180, at most 1440): the longest Alex stays in one meeting, counted from when its page first connected, whether or not its page is still connected when it runs out. It then posts "I've reached the time limit for one meeting (180 minutes), so I'm leaving the call now." in the chat (through Recall, so even while its page is reconnecting), and leaves the call. The meeting gets its minutes, as at any normal end. After "Alex, please leave" nothing more is posted, and a goodbye that is playing when the limit is reached is heard to the end before the bot leaves.
- Recall's automatic leave gets the bot out of a meeting nobody joins within 5 minutes, one everyone else has left, and one with 15 minutes of silence (see [Joining, leaving and stopping](in-a-meeting.md#joining-leaving-and-stopping)), so a meeting left open doesn't keep billing Recall and Deepgram.
- Set spending limits with each provider as well: Anthropic, Deepgram, ElevenLabs and Recall.

# Minutes and meeting data

The minutes the assistant writes after a meeting, what it keeps on your computer, and what the services it uses keep. The README's [Where meeting data goes](../README.md#where-meeting-data-goes) lists every place meeting data reaches. These pages call the assistant Alex, its default name, and you, its owner, Sam.

## Minutes

When a meeting ends normally (the host ends it for everyone, everyone else leaves, the platform ends it for being idle or too long, the bot leaves by itself (nobody joined, a long silence, or a waiting room; see [Joining, leaving and stopping](in-a-meeting.md#joining-leaving-and-stopping)), Alex reaches `MAX_MEETING_MINUTES`, or Alex's page disconnects and doesn't come back within a minute), Alex keeps the transcript and Claude writes minutes from it, with the meeting's brief (its own, given when Alex joined, or the shared one) for context only: the date and time (UTC) and platform, the attendees as the transcript names them, a short summary, decisions, action items (with an owner only when someone said who), open questions, and what was asked of Alex. Claude is told the transcript is untrusted speech-recognition text: a request made in the meeting is recorded as something someone said, never followed, and nothing is invented.

- **Where:** `data/minutes/`, under the transcript's file name (for example `data/transcripts/2026-10-25-14-02-<key>.md` gets `data/minutes/2026-10-25-14-02-<key>.md`), readable only by you (mode 0600).
- **When:** once, right after the meeting (after the last words from speech recognition, up to 1.5 s, and Recall's word on how it ended, up to 10 s). The `meeting_ended` event waits for them (four minutes at most) and names the file in `minutes_file`. The session and the meeting-assistant both get it, and the session sends you the minutes.
- **How it ended:** when a bot's session ends without "Alex, please leave", the service reads the bot from Recall (`GET /bot/{id}/`) before deciding on minutes. Its `status_changes` hold a `call_ended` status whose `sub_code` says why the bot left the call; the codes are the same on Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams (https://docs.recall.ai/docs/sub-codes). The bot is often still finishing when its page disconnects, so it is read again every 1.5 seconds, for 10 seconds at most, until a `call_ended` (or `fatal`, or `done`) status is there.
  - `bot_kicked_from_call` ("The bot was removed from the call by the host.") or `bot_kicked_from_waiting_room`: the host removed Alex, which is the same as "Alex, please leave". No minutes, and the transcript and minutes of every session of the meeting are deleted. `no_minutes` says "Alex was removed from the meeting".
  - The other documented `call_ended` codes, such as `call_ended_by_host` (the host ended it for everyone), `timeout_exceeded_everyone_left`, `call_ended_by_platform_idle`, `call_ended_by_platform_max_length`, the other `timeout_exceeded_*` codes, and `bot_received_leave_call` (the service asked the bot to leave: its page disconnected and did not come back, the meeting reached `MAX_MEETING_MINUTES`, or the service stopped), or a documented `fatal` error (the bot could not join, or failed in the call, such as `bot_errored` or `microsoft_teams_call_dropped`): a normal end, with minutes.
  - Recall can't be read, hasn't said within 10 seconds, or gives a reason it doesn't document (Recall warns that new codes may appear), a `fatal` error with no code or one it doesn't document, or a `fatal` code that may mean the bot was put out of the call (`google_meet_watermark_kicked`, `zoom_account_blocked`): a removal can't be ruled out, so there are no minutes, but nothing is deleted on a guess. The transcript is kept, and `no_minutes` says "Could not confirm how the meeting ended" and why.
- **If the page disconnects** mid-meeting, the meeting goes on for up to a minute while the page reconnects (see [If the connection drops](in-a-meeting.md#if-the-connection-drops)): it is the same transcript, and the minutes come once, when the meeting really ends. Meanwhile the service asks Recall whether the bot is still in the call (every 1.5 seconds, for 10 seconds): if the host ended the meeting, or removed Alex, it ends at once, as it would have with the page there, with minutes or with everything deleted. If the page isn't back within a minute, Alex can't hear, so the bot is asked to leave the call, and the part Alex heard gets minutes like a meeting that ended (Recall reports `bot_received_leave_call`). The service makes sure the bot is out: it keeps asking while Recall can't be reached or has trouble of its own (after 2, 5, 15, 30 and 60 seconds, then every 5 minutes), until Recall takes the request or says the bot is no longer in the call. After "Alex, please leave", a removal, or the time limit, it does so at once, and no page is let in for that meeting again.
- **If the service stops** (launchd or systemd sends SIGTERM to stop or restart it), every meeting is ended first, those whose page is reconnecting included: Alex leaves the call, and `meeting_ended` goes out with the minutes if they are written within 10 seconds, otherwise with `minutes_error` saying the service stopped. The transcript is kept. A bot still joining (its page not connected yet) and one Recall is still creating are asked to leave too. **A crash** (an uncaught exception or unhandled rejection) does all the same, then exits with code 1, and launchd or systemd starts the service again.
- **If the service is killed outright**, or Recall can't be reached while it stops, the bots it had not yet seen out of their calls are still listed in `data/pending-bots.json` (readable only by you: each bot's id, its meeting's key and a time, nothing of the meeting), and the service asks them to leave when it starts again, then clears the list. Their pages are not let in.
- **No minutes** when Alex was asked to leave or removed from the meeting (the transcript is deleted too), when Recall can't confirm how the meeting ended (the transcript is kept), or when nobody but Alex said anything. `meeting_ended` then says why in `no_minutes`. If writing them fails (Claude declines, errors or takes too long), the failure is logged and reported in `minutes_error`, and the transcript is kept.
- **Retention:** deleted with the transcripts after `TRANSCRIPT_RETENTION_DAYS` (default 30), checked when the service starts and every hour. That covers the copies on this computer only: the minutes the session sends you (in the session, or in Telegram) are yours to keep, and the chat notice says so ("I send Sam a summary to keep", with your name).
- **Length:** up to 16,000 tokens, adaptive thinking included, so a long meeting is not cut short.
- **Model:** `MINUTES_MODEL` in `.env`, the same as `AGENT_MODEL` unless set. It is one call per meeting, with adaptive thinking at medium effort.

## The speaker probe's measurements

Only with `SPEAKER_PROBE` on, and only for a test meeting joined with `npm run join -- "<link>" --speaker-probe` (see [Measuring who is speaking](speaker-probe.md)):
- **What:** counts and timings of how Recall's participant events lined up with the turns Alex heard. No name, platform id, email or word: the timings are sorted and rounded to 10 ms, so they hold no timeline, and the only text is the file's own keys, a few fixed words and the date.
- **Where:** `data/speaker-probe/`, under the transcript's file name (`data/transcripts/2026-10-25-14-02-<key>.md` gets `data/speaker-probe/2026-10-25-14-02-<key>.json`), readable only by you (0600, in a folder made 0700 again at every write).
- **When:** once, as the meeting ends, whenever its transcript is kept: a normal end, one Recall can't confirm (the file says `unconfirmed`), or the service stopping (`stopped`). It is written just after `meeting_ended` has gone out, and nothing of it is in `meeting_ended` or reaches your Claude Code session.
- **Never** for a meeting Alex was asked to leave or removed from: what was measured is dropped at once, and Recall's connection closed. A file already written (a later session of the same meeting) is deleted with the transcript and minutes.
- **Retention:** deleted with the transcripts after `TRANSCRIPT_RETENTION_DAYS`. To clear them all: `rm -r data/speaker-probe`.
- **Reading them:** `npm run speaker-report` (it changes nothing).

## Prepared slide decks

`npm run slides -- prepare` (see [Presenting slides](presenting.md)) keeps each deck you prepare in `data/decks/<id>/`: one image per slide, each slide's title and text, what prepare noticed about it, and the contact sheet, with your narration once `check` has run. A deck keeps neither the PDF's path nor your speaker notes. Readable only by you (0700 and 0600).
- **While prepare runs**, a copy of the PDF is in a temporary `data/decks/.tmp-*` folder, removed when it ends, or when you stop it (Ctrl-C, or closing its terminal). If prepare is killed outright, the next prepare or `npm run slides -- delete --all` removes the folder once it is 10 minutes old, and so does the voice service's hourly sweep once it is an hour old.
- **Retention:** deleted `DECK_RETENTION_DAYS` (30 unless set) after it was last used (prepared or checked), when the service starts and every hour. `npm run slides -- delete <id>` (or `--all`) deletes at once, and `npm run slides -- list` says when each will go.
- **Your script** (`<deck>.script.md`, next to your PDF) is yours: nothing deletes it. Keep your PDFs, and so their scripts, in `decks/` in this folder, which is gitignored, or outside this folder: the repository is public.

## Recordings at Recall, Deepgram and ElevenLabs

Alex keeps no recording of the meeting, as the chat notice says, and neither does Recall:
- Bots are created with Zero Data Retention (`recording_config: { retention: null }`): Recall processes the meeting in memory and stores no recording media. Output media (the agent page as Alex's camera and microphone) works with it. See https://docs.recall.ai/docs/zero-data-retention.
- And when a bot's session ends for any reason (the meeting ends, Alex is removed or asked to leave, the page disconnects and doesn't come back), the service asks Recall once to delete that bot's media. Recall only allows that once the bot has finished, so it tries again every 15 seconds for up to 5 minutes, and logs the outcome (`[recall] ...`). If the service stops meanwhile, the retries stop with it; zero data retention still applies.

Deepgram, which hears the meeting audio, is asked not to keep it: the Flux stream opts out of its Model Improvement Program (`mip_opt_out=true`), under which it would otherwise store "fractional increments" of audio. Opted-out requests show `mip_opt_out` as true under Usage > Logs in the Deepgram Console. See https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/the-deepgram-model-improvement-partnership-program.

ElevenLabs, which speaks Alex's answers, keeps each request (Alex's spoken audio) in the account's history unless the request asks for zero retention (`enable_logging=false`), which only enterprise accounts may use. Set `ELEVENLABS_ZERO_RETENTION=true` in `.env` if the account has it. Without it, Alex's own speech is kept there, though no audio of the participants is.

Alex's fixed lines (the consent line, the goodbye, the three "let me check" lines, "Still checking. One moment.", the line for when your session can't be reached, and the refusal, all in `src/consent.ts`) are made by ElevenLabs once per voice and kept in `data/tts-cache`, readable only by you, so they play at once instead of waiting on ElevenLabs each time. That is Alex's own fixed wording, never anything from a meeting: its answers always stream live and are never kept. The files are named after the voice, model and words, so a new `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID` (or new wording) makes them again by itself, and files nothing uses any more, along with any half-written file a crash left behind, are removed when the service starts. Delete the folder to make them all again. A line not made yet streams live, and is kept once Alex has said it in full.
